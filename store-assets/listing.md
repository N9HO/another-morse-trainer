## Full description

Learn to copy Morse code (CW) by ear, the way real operators do.

Another Morse Trainer teaches you with the proven Koch method: it starts with
two characters and adds the next one only when you're ready, building real
instant recognition instead of counting dots and dashes.

TWENTY WAYS TO TRAIN

Learn the characters
• Journey: a leveled path from letters through punctuation, prosigns, Q-codes,
  abbreviations and words to call signs, with an unlock map and saved progress.
• Characters: the Koch-method ladder through A-Z and 0-9.
• Confusion Drill: targeted reps on the exact pairs you keep mixing up.

Copy real content
• Common Words: copy whole ham-radio words at speed, or bring your own list.
• Abbreviations and Q-Codes: the CW shorthand you'll hear on the air.
• Prosigns: run-together signals like <AR> and <SK>.
• Short Stories: 32 bundled fables, longer classics with a
  bookmark that keeps your place, or today's real news headlines, hidden until
  you decode them.

Build speed
• Head Copy: hold a whole word in your head, then grade your own recall.
• Type It: free recall with no multiple choice to lean on.
• QRQ Speed: high-speed copy at 35, 40, 50 or 60 WPM.
• Rapid Fire: call signs, words, number groups or states sent back to back.

Get on the air
• Pileup Runner: call CQ and pull callsigns out of a simulated pileup, with
  QSB, QRN, cut numbers, busted calls and a live log.
• Contest: timed runs of five real weekly CW events (K1USN SST, ICWC MST,
  CWops CWT, NCCC Sprint, ARRL Field Day) with a live score and scorecard.
• Code Exam: the classic FCC/ARRL-style copy test at 5, 13, or 20 WPM.
• Repeater: live CW with real operators over the Vail network.

Send, decode and look up
• Sending Practice: key it back on a touch key or a hardware MIDI key, with
  live decode of what you actually sent.
• Sending Drills: printable practice sheets built from what you've studied.
• CW Decoder: point the microphone at a rig or a WebSDR and read received
  Morse as text.
• Reference: a tap-to-hear chart of prosigns, Q-codes, abbreviations, ham
  lingo and cut numbers.
• Listen & Learn: hands-free, eyes-free practice: hear the code, then the
  answer spoken aloud. Keeps playing with the screen locked, so you can learn
  while walking or driving.

BUILT FOR REAL PRACTICE
• Adjustable character speed up to 60 WPM, Farnsworth spacing, and sidetone
  pitch.
• Answer by tapping, typing, speaking, or keying it back.
• Speak your answers instead of tapping (optional, uses the microphone). It
  learns your corrections over time.
• Hardware key support: USB and Bluetooth LE MIDI keys, plus full Vail Adapter
  input and output.
• An optional background noise floor, on quietly by default, so Bluetooth
  earbuds don't sleep through the first character of a word. Turn it up and it
  doubles as band noise to copy through.
• A daily practice reminder to keep your streak alive.
• Progress tracking: practice streak, accuracy, your fastest copy, most-confused
  pairs, performance by speed band, and a per-character recognition-speed chart
  so you can see exactly which characters are slowing you down.
• Phone and tablet layouts.

No accounts. No ads. No tracking. Everything stays on your device.

## "What's new" (release notes for 1.10.1)
The soft keyboard now gets out of your way in the QSO Simulator. Entering your
call sign left the keyboard up with no way to close it, covering the Start
button so the only escape was the back button. Tap anywhere off the field, or
press the keyboard's Done key, and it closes; the setup screen also sits above
the keyboard now, so Start stays reachable either way.

Carried over from 1.10, if you are coming from 1.9:

A fresh install starts at 33 WPM character speed, matching the Apple app. The
Koch method needs characters arriving too fast to count, and 18 WPM was slow
enough to tick off the dits and dahs. Nothing changes for anyone already
training. Drop below 33 and the settings screen says what you are trading away.

The QSO simulator's exchange accepts what operators actually send. A signal
report typed in front of the exchange no longer counts as a miscopy, including
in SST, CWT, MST, Sprint and Field Day, which never send one, whether you write
it 5NN or 599, spaced or run together. Cut numbers are read wherever a digit is
expected, so a Field Day class copied as "NB" is a 9B.
